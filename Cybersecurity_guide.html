AWARENESS

Phishing & Social Engineering

Cybercriminals don't always break through walls — they often walk right through the front door because you held it open. Phishing and social engineering exploit human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities. Understanding the tactics is your first line of defence.

THREAT LEVEL
HIGH RISK
By the numbers: Over 3.4 billion phishing emails are sent every day. In the Philippines, phishing is the #1 reported cybercrime — commonly targeting GCash, BPI, Metrobank, and Shopee users. The FBI's IC3 reports phishing as the most common attack type for 5 consecutive years.
Types of Phishing Attacks
01
Email Phishing
Mass-sent emails impersonating banks, shipping companies, or government agencies. They include urgent calls to action and fake links to credential-harvesting sites.
02
Smishing (SMS)
Text messages claiming your package is held, your account is locked, or you've won a prize. Often include shortened URLs that disguise the real destination.
03
Vishing (Voice)
Scammers call pretending to be tech support, bank agents, or government officials. They use urgency and authority to extract OTPs, PINs, or card numbers over the phone.
04
Spear Phishing
Highly targeted attacks using personal details scraped from social media — your name, employer, colleagues — to craft a believable, personalized deception.
05
QR Code Scams
Printed or digital QR codes placed on posters, menus, or fake payment terminals that redirect to phishing sites — bypassing URL-visibility checks entirely.
06
Malicious Files
PDFs, Excel sheets, and Word docs that look legitimate but contain hidden macros or scripts that silently install malware the moment you enable editing.
The Phishing Attack Chain
01
Lure — Creating False Urgency
You receive an alarming message: "Your GCash account is suspended" or "You've won ₱50,000!" The goal is panic or excitement — both bypass rational thinking and trigger impulsive action.
INITIAL CONTACT
02
Hook — The Fake Destination
A link leads to a pixel-perfect clone of a legitimate site. Attackers use typosquatted domains like "gcash-security.com" or "bpi-verify.net". The site may even have HTTPS to appear trustworthy.
REDIRECT
03
Catch — Credential Harvest
You enter your username, password, or OTP on the fake site. This data is sent to the attacker in real time — sometimes while you're being redirected to the real site to mask suspicion.
CRITICAL
04
Exploit — Immediate Account Takeover
Within minutes, funds are transferred, accounts are sold on dark web markets, or identities are used for further fraud. Stolen credentials are often bundled and resold to multiple threat actors simultaneously.
BREACH
Social Engineering Mind Tricks
AUTHORITY
Impersonating a bank official, government agent (BIR, SSS), or tech company executive to create pressure to comply without questioning.
URGENCY
"Your account will be permanently deleted in 24 hours." Time pressure prevents careful thinking, pushing victims to act before they can reason.
SCARCITY
"Only 3 prizes remaining!" Exploits fear of missing out (FOMO) to drive clicks and form submissions.
SOCIAL PROOF
"10,000 people have already claimed theirs." Fake testimonials and inflated numbers make scams appear legitimate and safe to engage with.
RECIPROCITY
Offering something free — a gift card, discount, or useful tool — to make victims feel obligated to provide personal information in return.
FAMILIARITY
Using your name, referencing a mutual friend, or mentioning recent purchases to create a false sense of trust and legitimacy before the ask.
Red Flags to Watch For
Urgent or threatening language Misspelled or unusual domain names Generic greetings ("Dear User") No HTTPS padlock on sensitive pages Unexpected file attachments Any request for OTP, PIN, or password
Golden Rule: No legitimate bank, government agency, or company will ever ask for your OTP, full PIN, or password over phone, SMS, or email — ever. If someone requests it, it is a scam. Hang up, delete the message, and report it immediately.
ONLINE SCAMS

Online Scams

Digital scams evolve constantly, exploiting new platforms and timely events. From fake online shops to romance fraud, scammers invest time and creativity into making their traps feel real. Knowing the playbook makes you nearly immune.

THREAT LEVEL
HIGH RISK
Philippine Context: The PNP-ACG reported over ₱1.2 billion lost to online scams in 2023 alone. Investment scams, online shopping fraud, and romance scams top the list. Victims are increasingly targeted on Facebook, TikTok, and Telegram.
Common Scam Types
01
Investment & "Negosyo" Scams
Promises of 20–100% weekly returns through crypto trading bots, forex signals, or online business systems. These are Ponzi or pyramid schemes — early participants get paid using money from new recruits until it collapses. Red flag: guaranteed returns, recruitment bonuses, pressure to invest quickly.
CRITICAL
02
Fake Online Shops
Websites or social media stores that collect payment then never ship. They use stolen product photos, fabricated reviews, and unverifiable contact info. Always check for a physical address and use payment methods with buyer protection — never direct GCash transfers to strangers.
HIGH
03
Romance Scams (Love Scamming)
Scammers build fake emotional relationships over weeks or months using stolen photos of attractive people. Once trust is established, they invent a crisis and request money. They will never meet in person and always have an excuse to avoid video calls.
HIGH
04
Job & Work-From-Home Scams
"Earn ₱5,000/day liking posts!" — tasks that require an initial "activation fee" to unlock earnings. Victims pay but never receive payouts. Legitimate employers never charge fees to start working. Other variants target OFW applicants through fake POEA job placements.
MEDIUM
05
Tech Support Scams
A browser pop-up warns your PC is infected and displays a hotline number. Calling connects you to scammers who ask for remote access and payment to fix a non-existent problem. Microsoft, Apple, and Google never initiate unsolicited support calls.
MEDIUM
The Scam Verification Checklist
✓
Search the seller/company name + "scam" or "legit" before transacting
✓
Never pay via direct bank/GCash transfer to personal accounts for unknown sellers
✓
Verify business registration with the SEC or DTI using their official websites
✓
Video call the person before sending money — scammers avoid live video
✓
Report suspected scams to PNP-ACG: ecomplaint.pnp.gov.ph
If it sounds too good to be true, it is. Extraordinary claims — massive returns, free prizes, dream jobs — require extraordinary evidence. When pressured to decide quickly, the correct answer is always to walk away and verify first.
MALWARE

Malware & Ransomware

Malware is malicious software designed to infiltrate, damage, or gain unauthorized access to devices. Once installed, it can spy on you, encrypt your files for ransom, steal banking credentials, or turn your device into a robot soldier in a criminal botnet.

THREAT LEVEL
CRITICAL
Scale of the threat: Ransomware attacks cost businesses globally $20 billion in 2023. A ransomware attack occurs every 11 seconds. Hospitals, schools, and government agencies are prime targets — including several in the Philippines that have suffered significant data breaches.
Malware Types Explained
01
Ransomware
Encrypts all your files and demands payment (usually in cryptocurrency) for the decryption key. Even after paying, files are often not recovered. Spreads via email attachments, malicious downloads, and unpatched software vulnerabilities.
02
Trojans
Software that appears legitimate — a game mod, a free app, a crack for paid software — but contains hidden malicious code. Once installed, it silently grants attackers access to your system or installs additional malware.
03
Spyware & Keyloggers
Secretly records everything you type — passwords, messages, credit card numbers — and sends it to the attacker. Some variants take periodic screenshots or activate webcams without your knowledge.
04
Adware & Browser Hijackers
Floods your browser with ads, redirects searches to fake engines, and installs extensions without permission. While less dangerous, it slows your device and can be a gateway for worse malware.
05
Rootkits
Designed to hide deep within the operating system, making them nearly invisible to standard antivirus tools. They give attackers persistent, privileged access to your system over long periods.
06
Worms
Self-replicating programs that spread across networks without user interaction. A single infected device on a network can compromise all connected machines within hours.
How to Stay Protected
✓
Keep your OS, browser, and apps updated — patches close the vulnerabilities malware exploits
✓
Only download software from official sources (App Store, Google Play, official websites)
✓
Never install pirated software or game cracks — they are the #1 malware delivery vector
✓
Use a reputable antivirus and run regular scans (Windows Defender is sufficient for most users)
✓
Back up important files to an external drive or cloud storage — the ultimate ransomware protection
The 3-2-1 Backup Rule: Keep 3 copies of your data, on 2 different storage types, with 1 copy offsite or in the cloud. This ensures ransomware can never permanently destroy your files.
HOW HACKERS OPERATE

How Hackers Get You

Understanding the attacker's perspective is one of the most powerful defensive tools available. Hackers follow predictable patterns — a kill chain of reconnaissance, intrusion, and exploitation. Know the chain, and you can break it.

THREAT LEVEL
EXTREME
The Hacker's Kill Chain
01
Reconnaissance — They Research You First
Before attacking, hackers gather intelligence: your name, employer, email, phone number, social media posts, and personal connections — all publicly available on Facebook, LinkedIn, and Instagram. This is called OSINT (Open Source Intelligence). Your public profile is a hacker's research report.
PASSIVE
02
Credential Stuffing
Billions of username/password combinations from past data breaches are available on the dark web. Hackers use automated tools to try these credentials on banking, email, and shopping sites. If you reuse passwords, one breach exposes every account. Check haveibeenpwned.com to see if your email has been leaked.
AUTOMATED
03
Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attacks
On public WiFi (cafés, malls, airports), hackers can position themselves between you and the network — intercepting all traffic. They can read unencrypted data, steal session cookies, and redirect you to fake login pages. Always use a VPN on public networks.
NETWORK
04
SIM Swapping
Hackers call your mobile carrier, impersonate you using personal details (birthday, address), and convince the agent to transfer your phone number to their SIM. Once they control your number, they receive all your OTPs and 2FA codes — giving them access to every account linked to your phone number.
CRITICAL
05
Account Takeover & Lateral Movement
Once inside one account, hackers use it to pivot to others — accessing stored payment methods, sending phishing messages to your contacts, and resetting passwords using email recovery. A single compromised email account can cascade into total loss of your digital identity.
BREACH
How to Break the Kill Chain
USE 2FA
Enable two-factor authentication on every account. Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Aegis) rather than SMS-based OTPs which are vulnerable to SIM swap.
UNIQUE PASSWORDS
Use a different password for every site. A password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password) generates and stores them securely so you only remember one master password.
LOCK YOUR SIM
Contact Globe, Smart, or DITO to add a PIN or verbal password to your account — preventing anyone from swapping your SIM without that code.
AUDIT YOUR PRIVACY
Review Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn privacy settings. Remove public birthdate, hometown, and phone number — the data hackers use in OSINT and social engineering.
USE A VPN
On public WiFi, a VPN encrypts your traffic, preventing man-in-the-middle interception. Choose reputable providers like ProtonVPN or Mullvad — avoid free VPNs which may sell your data.
PATCH EVERYTHING
Enable automatic updates on your phone, computer, and browser. The majority of successful exploits target known vulnerabilities that had patches available but weren't installed.
Minimum viable security: Unique passwords + 2FA authenticator app + automatic updates covers over 90% of attack vectors that successfully compromise everyday users. You don't need to be a security expert — just consistent.
PASSWORD SECURITY

Password Security

Passwords are the keys to your digital life. Yet "123456" remains the world's most common password. Strong, unique passwords combined with a password manager form an impenetrable defence against the majority of account compromises.

Cracking speeds: A modern GPU can crack an 8-character password using brute force in under 8 hours. A 12-character password with mixed characters would take 3,000 years. Length is your biggest ally.
Password Strength Guide
Weak: Common Words & Numbers
Passwords like "password123", "abc123", or "manila2024" are in every hacker's dictionary file and cracked instantly. Never use personal info: birthdays, pet names, or your street address.
CRITICAL RISK
Better: Random Characters
Something like "Tr0ub4dor&3" is stronger, but still memorable patterns are vulnerable. The real issue is that most people reuse the same strong password across multiple sites — negating the strength entirely.
MODERATE
Best: Passphrases + Unique Per Site
A passphrase like "correct-horse-battery-staple" is long, memorable, and exponentially harder to crack. Better yet: use a password manager to generate a unique 20+ character random string for every site.
RECOMMENDED
Recommended Password Managers
Bitwarden
Free, open-source, and audited. Available on all platforms. Stores passwords encrypted in the cloud. The best free option for most users.
1Password
Premium but polished. Excellent family sharing features. Includes travel mode to hide sensitive vaults at border crossings.
KeePassXC
Fully offline and open-source. Your password database never touches the internet. Ideal for maximum privacy-conscious users.
Enable 2FA everywhere: Even with a perfect password, enable two-factor authentication. Use an authenticator app like Google Authenticator or Aegis (Android) — not SMS, which can be intercepted. This single step stops 99.9% of automated account takeover attacks.
SAFE BROWSING

Safe Browsing

Every website you visit, every link you click, and every file you download is a potential entry point for attackers. Building smart browsing habits transforms your browser from a liability into a fortified gateway.

THREAT LEVEL
MEDIUM RISK
Did you know? Over 1.5 million new phishing websites are created every month. HTTPS does not mean a site is safe — it only means the connection is encrypted. Scam sites routinely use HTTPS. Always verify the domain itself, not just the padlock.
Browsing Threats to Know
01
Drive-By Downloads
Malware that installs automatically when you visit a compromised or malicious webpage — no click required. Keeping your browser and plugins updated is the primary defence.
02
Malvertising
Legitimate ad networks can serve malicious ads that redirect you to exploit pages or initiate downloads. Even reputable sites can unknowingly display these. An ad blocker (uBlock Origin) is effective protection.
03
Fake Browser Extensions
Extensions with names similar to popular tools that actually steal passwords, inject ads, or track browsing history. Install extensions only from official browser stores and review permissions carefully.
04
Typosquatting
Domains like 'gooogle.com' or 'facebok.com' that look like familiar sites but are controlled by attackers. One accidental typo can land you on a credential-harvesting clone.
05
Cookie Theft
Session cookies stored in your browser can be stolen via XSS attacks or malicious scripts, allowing attackers to impersonate you on websites without needing your password. Logging out of sites when done helps mitigate this.
06
Clipboard Hijacking
Malicious scripts on websites can swap crypto wallet addresses you copy to your clipboard with the attacker's address. Always double-check copied addresses before pasting in any financial transaction.
Essential Browser Security Settings
✓
Install uBlock Origin — the most effective free ad and malicious script blocker
✓
Enable DNS over HTTPS (DoH) in browser settings to encrypt DNS queries
✓
Set your browser to block third-party cookies and enable "Enhanced Tracking Protection"
✓
Regularly audit and remove browser extensions you no longer use
✓
Use Google Safe Browsing or similar tools to scan suspicious URLs before visiting
Browser recommendation: Firefox with uBlock Origin offers the best combination of privacy, security, and performance for most users. Brave browser is also a strong choice with built-in ad blocking and fingerprint protection. Avoid browsers that are themselves data-harvesting tools.
PRIVACY & DATA

Privacy & Your Data

Data is the new oil — and your personal information is extracted, packaged, and sold constantly. From the apps on your phone to the websites you visit, understanding who has your data and what they do with it is the first step in taking back control.

Philippine law: The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. 10173) gives Filipinos the right to know what personal data organizations collect, the right to correct inaccurate data, and the right to request deletion. The National Privacy Commission (NPC) handles complaints at privacy.gov.ph.
How Your Data Is Collected
01
App Permissions Overreach
Many free apps request access to your contacts, camera, microphone, location, and storage — far beyond what they need to function. A flashlight app doesn't need your contacts. Review app permissions regularly and revoke anything unnecessary.
COMMON
02
Browser Fingerprinting
Websites can identify you even without cookies by collecting your browser version, screen resolution, installed fonts, time zone, and dozens of other signals that form a unique fingerprint. This allows tracking across sites even in private/incognito mode.
PASSIVE
03
Data Broker Profiles
Data broker companies compile detailed profiles about you — your address history, income estimates, relatives, and online activity — and sell this to advertisers, insurers, and anyone willing to pay. You can request removal from major brokers, though it requires persistence.
ONGOING
04
Social Media Data Mining
Every like, share, and post trains algorithms about your political views, purchasing intentions, relationships, and vulnerabilities. This data is used to serve targeted ads and — in more sinister cases — micro-targeted disinformation.
HIGH
Privacy Protection Actions
USE SIGNAL
Signal is an end-to-end encrypted messenger that stores minimal metadata. For sensitive conversations, it's significantly more private than SMS, Messenger, or Viber.
PRIVATE EMAIL
ProtonMail and Tutanota offer end-to-end encrypted email stored in privacy-friendly jurisdictions. Use these for sensitive communications or as your primary email to reduce data exposure.
LIMIT APP DATA
Go to Settings → Apps → Permissions on your phone and audit what each app can access. Disable location access for apps that don't need it, and set location to 'only while using' where possible.
USE ALIASES
Services like SimpleLogin let you create unique email aliases for each website. If one leaks, you know exactly which service was breached, and you can disable that alias without changing your real email.
SEARCH PRIVATELY
DuckDuckGo and Brave Search don't build a profile of your searches or sell your query history. Switching from Google reduces the data volume that corporations hold about you.
KNOW YOUR RIGHTS
Under R.A. 10173, you can file a complaint with the NPC if a company mishandles your data. Document the incident and contact privacy.gov.ph — companies face fines and criminal penalties for violations.
Privacy is not about hiding: It's about control. You have the right to determine who can access information about your life, your habits, and your identity. Treating privacy as a default — not an exception — significantly reduces your attack surface for social engineering and targeted fraud.
MOBILE SECURITY

Mobile Security

Your smartphone is the most personal computer you own — it has your banking apps, your messages, your location, and your photos. It is also the most common target for cybercriminals in the Philippines. A compromised phone can mean losing everything.

THREAT LEVEL
HIGH RISK
Mobile-first attacks: Over 60% of digital fraud in Southeast Asia originates through mobile devices. GCash, Maya, and online banking apps are the primary targets. Most compromises happen through side-loaded APK files, SIM swaps, or stolen OTPs.
Mobile Threats Breakdown
01
Malicious APK Files
Android allows installing apps outside the Play Store via APK files — a feature widely abused to distribute banking trojans and spyware. Never install APKs from messaging apps, websites, or unknown sources. Stick to official app stores only.
02
Juice Jacking
Compromised USB charging ports in airports, malls, and cafés can install malware or steal data while your phone charges. Use your own wall charger or carry a USB data blocker ('USB condom') if you must use public ports.
03
Fake App Clones
Apps with names like 'GCash Pro', 'BPI Mobile Plus', or 'Shopee Deals' that mimic legitimate apps to steal login credentials. Always verify app publisher names and download counts before installing.
04
Screen Overlay Attacks
Malware that places an invisible layer over your banking app, capturing your PIN and credentials as you type them into what appears to be the legitimate app. Keep your Android security settings configured to 'Block overlays from unknown apps.'
Mobile Security Best Practices
✓
Enable biometric lock (fingerprint or face ID) and a strong PIN — never use pattern locks
✓
Enable Find My Device (Android) or Find My iPhone and set remote wipe capability
✓
Keep your phone's OS updated — security patches often address critical vulnerabilities
✓
Never root (Android) or jailbreak (iOS) your device — it removes the OS security model entirely
✓
Set up a SIM PIN under Settings → Security to prevent unauthorized SIM use if your phone is stolen
✓
Disable Bluetooth and WiFi when not in use — they can be used for proximity-based attacks
If your phone is stolen: Act within the first 30 minutes. Remotely lock and wipe via Find My Device. Immediately change passwords for your email and banking apps from another device. Contact your bank's emergency hotline to freeze linked accounts. File a report with the PNP — you'll need it for the bank.
INCIDENT RESPONSE

Incident Response

Even the most security-conscious person can be compromised. What separates a minor incident from a catastrophe is how quickly and correctly you respond. Speed matters — every minute of hesitation gives attackers more time to entrench and cause further damage.

Time is critical: Studies show attackers typically begin exploiting stolen credentials within 9 minutes of obtaining them. In phishing attacks targeting GCash, funds are frequently transferred within 2–5 minutes of credentials being entered. If you suspect a compromise, act immediately — don't wait to be sure.
If You've Been Phished
01
Disconnect Immediately
If you suspect malware was installed, disconnect the device from WiFi and mobile data before taking any other action. This cuts off the attacker's remote access channel and prevents further data exfiltration while you assess the situation.
FIRST
02
Change All Passwords From a Clean Device
Use a different, uncompromised device (a family member's phone, a library computer) to change passwords for email first, then banking and financial apps, then social media. Start with email — it controls password resets for everything else.
SECOND
03
Contact Your Bank Immediately
Call your bank's 24/7 hotline and explain you've been compromised. Request an immediate freeze on your account and flag any suspicious transactions. Banks can sometimes reverse unauthorized transfers if contacted within the transaction window — usually 24 hours.
THIRD
04
Enable 2FA on Recovered Accounts
Once you've regained control of your accounts, immediately enable two-factor authentication everywhere — preferably using an authenticator app, not SMS. This prevents the same attack from working again if credentials were captured.
FOURTH
05
File Reports with Authorities
Report to PNP-ACG (ecomplaint.pnp.gov.ph), BSP Consumer Affairs (for banking fraud), and the BSP's fintech complaint portal for e-wallet issues. Filing a report creates an official record, which can be crucial for bank reversals, insurance claims, and legal proceedings.
FIFTH
Warning Signs You've Been Compromised
UNKNOWN LOGINS
Receiving login notifications from unfamiliar locations or devices. Check your account's active sessions immediately and terminate anything you don't recognize.
UNEXPECTED OTPs
Receiving OTPs you didn't request means someone is actively trying to access your account with your credentials — act immediately. Change your password and contact the service.
MISSING FUNDS
Small unauthorized transactions often precede larger ones — attackers test with micro-transactions first. Enable transaction notifications on all banking apps and review statements weekly.
SLOW DEVICE
Sudden performance drops, battery draining much faster than normal, or unusual data usage spikes can indicate malware running background processes. Run a full antivirus scan and check data usage in Settings.
CONTACTS REPORT MESSAGES
Friends or family receiving suspicious messages from your accounts is a strong indicator of compromise. Your account is being used to phish your network — change passwords immediately.
LOCKED OUT
If you can't log in to an account you haven't changed the password for, an attacker may have already taken it over. Use account recovery immediately and contact the service's support team.
Don't be ashamed: Phishing and social engineering attacks are designed by professionals specifically to deceive smart, careful people. Falling for one doesn't mean you're careless — it means you were targeted. The most important thing is to respond quickly, document everything, and report it so others can be warned.
SAFETY CHECKLIST

Your Safety Checklist

Cybersecurity doesn't require a degree — just consistent habits. This checklist covers the essential actions every Filipino internet user should take to dramatically reduce their risk of falling victim to cybercrime.

Immediate Actions (Do These Today)
✓
Enable two-factor authentication on email, GCash, banking apps, and social media
✓
Check if your email has been in a data breach at haveibeenpwned.com
✓
Change any reused or weak passwords — especially for email and banking
✓
Review your Facebook privacy settings — set birthdate and phone number to "Only Me"
✓
Update your phone, computer OS, and all installed apps to the latest versions
✓
Install Bitwarden or another password manager and begin migrating your passwords
Ongoing Habits (Build These)
→
Never share OTPs with anyone — not even people claiming to be bank agents
→
Verify links before clicking — hover to preview the URL destination first
→
Download apps only from official stores; avoid APK files from random websites or chat messages
→
Use a VPN when connecting to public WiFi in cafés, malls, or airports
→
Back up important files regularly using the 3-2-1 rule
→
Enable transaction notifications on all banking and e-wallet apps — catch fraud early
→
Be skeptical of any message creating urgency, offering prizes, or requesting money transfers

Share what you know. Cybersecurity awareness is a community defence. Teach your family — especially parents and grandparents — about phishing, scams, and OTP protection. A single informed person in a household can protect everyone in it.
